How to move your ChatGPT memory to Claude (and what the built-in import leaves behind)

You have spent months teaching ChatGPT how you work: your preferences, your rules, your people, your projects. Switching to Claude normally means starting from zero, because each AI keeps what it knows about you inside its own product. There are two real ways to carry it across.

Disclosure: I build clonemy.ai, the tool in the second method below. The first method is Anthropic's own, and for a light move it may be all you need. Here is both, with the limits stated plainly.

First, know what ChatGPT actually holds

ChatGPT keeps two kinds of memory. Saved memories are facts it stored when you asked it to, or judged worth keeping. Reference chat history means it draws on past conversations to personalize replies without keeping a separate list. Some accounts show an auto-updating memory summary instead.

You can review all of it under Settings, Personalization, Memory: read the saved memories, delete individual ones, and correct or delete the summary. Do this before any move. A move copies whatever is there, including the stale parts.

One dead end to avoid: Settings, Data controls, Export data sends you a ZIP of your conversations as JSON. That is an archive of your chats, not your memory. OpenAI documents that memories, custom instructions, and settings do not transfer through the export.

Method 1: Claude's built-in import

Anthropic ships a memory import, the first of its kind:

  1. In Claude, open Settings, Memory, and choose Start import.
  2. Claude gives you a prompt. Run that prompt in ChatGPT.
  3. ChatGPT answers with a prose summary of what it knows about you.
  4. Paste the answer back into Claude. Claude extracts memory entries from it, which you can review and keep or drop.

It takes a few minutes and it is a genuine start. Its limits, in Anthropic's own framing:

  • Imports are labeled experimental and still in active development, and Anthropic warns Claude may not always successfully incorporate imported memories.
  • Claude's memory is designed to focus on work, and imported personal details unrelated to work may not be retained.
  • It is a one-time copy of prose. Your rules arrive as extracts, not verbatim, and there is no check afterward that shows you what actually landed.

If your ChatGPT memory is short and work-focused, stop here. You are done.

Method 2: the capsule route

A capsule is one structured file that carries the whole relationship, not a prose summary: who the AI is to you, your operating rules in your own words, your preferences as specifics, your people, your projects and open loops with prices and deadlines, and your calibration history, meaning the corrections you already taught once and should not have to teach again.

  1. Extract. Send the extraction request to ChatGPT. It answers with a structured document about you, specifics rather than summaries, with gaps marked instead of invented.
  2. Compile. Bring the document to the compiler at clonemy.ai. It runs entirely in your browser tab, nothing is uploaded, and out comes one file, capsule.md. A Chrome extension does the same job inside the chat tab if you prefer.
  3. Install. Paste the capsule into Claude as messages: use the compiler's “Send it as messages” view and send each numbered part as its own message, in order. The capsule's opening rides at the head of the first part, so Claude adopts it as its standing configuration and announces the installation. The capsule.md file is the copy you keep, the capsule's container and storage; its contents travel as your messages, never as an attachment.
  4. Verify. The capsule ends with a three-question self-check that the new AI answers from the capsule alone: who it is to you, your never-do rules in your own words, and your open loops. If it cannot answer, the transfer is not done, and you find out before you rely on it.

What survives this route that the import does not promise: your rules verbatim, your people and open loops as structured sections rather than prose, material the work-focused filter would have dropped, and the calibration history. In testing, a capsule installed into a fresh Claude passed a 10-point parity probe on rules, people, and open loops, answering from the capsule alone.

Questions people ask about this move

Can you transfer your chat history itself? No. Neither method moves conversations as conversations. The export ZIP is an archive you keep, not something Claude ingests as history.

Does the move keep syncing afterward? No. Both methods are snapshots. When your rules or projects change, the capsule route takes about two minutes to redo: recompile and paste it in again. The file is yours, so the redo is cheap.

Which method should you pick? The built-in import for a quick, light, work-focused move. The capsule when what your AI knows about you is long, personal, rule-heavy, or worth verifying.
