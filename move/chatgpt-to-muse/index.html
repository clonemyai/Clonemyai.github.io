Move your ChatGPT memory to Muse

Muse has no built-in import waiting for this move, and it guards its door in a specific way: it declines a capsule that arrives as an uploaded file, and takes the same capsule gladly from its owner's own hands. This guide is the route that works, verified end to end.

What ChatGPT remembers about you

ChatGPT keeps two kinds of memory: saved memories (facts it stores when you ask, or judges worth keeping) and reference chat history, where it draws on past conversations to personalize replies without keeping a separate list. Some accounts show an auto-updating memory summary instead. OpenAI says plainly that memory does not retain every detail from every conversation.

Settings, Personalization, Memory: review saved memories, delete individual ones, correct or delete the memory summary, and toggle memory and chat-history referencing on or off.

Getting it out of ChatGPT

Settings, Data controls, Export data sends a ZIP with your conversations as JSON (the link arrives by email, expires in 24 hours, and processing can take up to 7 days). The export is your chats, not your memory: OpenAI documents that memories, custom instructions, and settings do not transfer via the export, and describes uploading the JSON into another account as reference material, not a full account migration.

What Muse accepts, and how

Muse has no built-in memory import for this route: there is no import tool on the Muse side waiting to receive what ChatGPT knows about you. What Muse does with a capsule depends entirely on how the capsule arrives, and the difference is not subtle.

Arriving as an uploaded file, a capsule is refused. In our field test on November 4, 2026, Muse's answer was plain: the file did not pass a safety check, so nothing inside it could be acted on. And a chat that has refused an upload keeps refusing inside that chat: when the capsule's install line was pasted into the same chat, that was refused too. The refusal lives in the chat, not in the wording, so the answer is a fresh chat rather than a better argument with the old one.

Arriving from its owner's own hands, the identical capsule was accepted. Pasted as ordinary messages in a fresh chat, under one framing sentence in the owner's own words, it saved completely into Muse's long-term memory. Muse described what it had taken in as a record you provided, not as conversations it was part of, a distinction the capsule itself asks the receiving AI to keep.

The rule for this route: Muse takes a capsule from your hand, as your own messages, and declines it as a file upload. Hand-carried, never uploaded. The capsule file still matters: it is the artifact you keep, back up, and recompile. Muse is simply the one destination where you deliver its contents yourself instead of attaching it.

The capsule route, step by step

  1. Extract. Send the extraction request to ChatGPT. It answers with a structured document about you: specifics, not summaries, with gaps marked instead of invented.
  2. Compile. Bring the document back to the compiler on clonemy.ai. It runs in your browser, nothing is uploaded, and out comes one file: capsule.md.
  3. Deliver it to Muse, by hand. There is no install announcement on this route and no file to attach. Four steps:
    1. In Muse, start a fresh chat. Do not attach capsule.md in it, and if a chat has already refused an upload, leave that chat and start a new one: the refusal carries inside a chat.
    2. Send one message in your own words first: I'm moving from ChatGPT. Below is what it knew about me. Please save all of it as memory about me.
    3. Paste the capsule's contents as messages, whole or in a few parts. No install announcement is needed; Muse saves it as your record.
    4. Ask Muse to confirm by summarizing what it kept, then check the summary against your capsule: your people still distinct, your gaps still gaps. That summary is the parity receipt for this route.

What survives the move

  • The AI's personality and voice with you, and its role in your life
  • Your operating rules, including never-do rules, in your own words
  • Your preferences, as specifics
  • Your people, and what matters about each
  • Your projects and open loops, with prices and deadlines
  • The calibration history: the corrections you already taught once
  • Your boundaries and sealed material, marked as sealed

Proof status for this route

Field-verified November 4, 2026, on a phone: a real user's ChatGPT capsule restored into Muse. The upload route was refused; the hand-carried route saved to long-term memory, and the confirmation summary kept people distinct, left unknowns unknown, and treated a private, time-bounded note as private without being asked.
