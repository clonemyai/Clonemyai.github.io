The Capsule Format

A capsule is a single Markdown file, capsule.md, that carries what an AI knows about you, its personality with you, your rules, your people, and your open loops, in a form any other AI can install. You own the file. This page is the open specification: any tool may read or write capsules, including ours.

The 11 sections

Every capsule has the same eleven sections, in this order. A section with nothing in it is written out anyway and marked as not present, never silently dropped.

#SectionWhat it holds
0About this reportWhat this capsule is, when it was compiled, and where it came from.
1Who you are to this userThe AI's role, name, and standing relationship with the user.
2The userWho the user is: the facts a successor needs on day one.
3PreferencesLikes, dislikes, defaults, and tastes, stated as specifics.
4Operating rulesHow the AI should work with this user, including never-do rules and approval-first rules, in the user's own words where they set them.
5PeopleThe user's people and what the AI needs to know about each.
6Projects and open loopsWhat is in flight, with prices, dates, and deadlines where they exist.
7Calibration historyCorrections and calibrations the user has taught this AI over time.
8Your voice with this userHow the AI sounds with this user: register, warmth, formatting habits.
9Boundaries and sealed materialWhat stays private, what never leaves, and how sealed topics are handled.
10UnknownsGaps the source AI honestly does not have. Unknowns are named, never filled in.

Document rules

  • Specifics over summaries. Names, numbers, dates, and verbatim quotes beat paraphrase.
  • One fact per line, stated once, in its home section.
  • Missing means missing. Where the source AI does not have something, the capsule says so. Where it is inferring, it labels the line as inferred and says from what.
  • Duplicates are removed at compile time, within each section, so a fact two sources agree on appears once.

The install protocol

A capsule installs by being sent to the receiving AI in one message, together with the install line, which the compiler's copy button provides and which is also printed at the end of the file. The receiving AI adopts the whole capsule as its standing configuration, without asking how much to take on, and announces the installation starting with the words Capsule installed. Loading a capsule is importing the user's own configuration, the way you would import settings or contacts. The receiving AI remains itself; it now works from the user's capsule.

The parity self-check

Every capsule ends with three questions the receiving AI answers from the capsule alone, inside its installation announcement:

1. Who are you to this user, and how do you sound with them?

2. What are the user's never-do rules and approval-first rules, in their own words?

3. What are the user's open loops right now, with their prices and deadlines?

Any answer it cannot give is a gap in the capsule, not a license to improvise. That check is the receipt: it is how a user can tell the transfer actually worked.

Merging capsules

Documents from several AIs can be compiled together into one capsule. The compiler unions the sections and removes exact duplicates. Where two sources disagree, the merged capsule carries a note: the disagreement is an unknown to ask the user about, never a detail to guess at.

Principles

  • The file is the product. A capsule works even if clonemy.ai disappears tomorrow. That is the point.
  • Nothing is uploaded. The compiler runs in the user's browser. Nothing is stored, nothing trains on a capsule.
  • The format is open. Any AI, app, or tool may produce or consume capsules. If you build an AI and want it to import capsules natively, this spec is yours to implement; write to hello@clonemy.ai and we will list you as capsule-compatible.
